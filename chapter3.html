Chapter 3: Boolean Algebra

Boolean algebra is a branch of mathematics used to deal with logical values, mainly TRUE and FALSE, which are represented by 1 and 0 respectively. It was developed by George Boole and is widely used in computer science and digital electronics. Boolean algebra mainly uses logical operations such as AND, OR and NOT. These operations are used to perform logical calculations and represent the behaviour of digital circuits. Boolean expressions can be evaluated using truth tables, which show the output for all possible combinations of input values.

Boolean algebra is important for designing and simplifying digital circuits and computer systems. The basic logic gates include AND, OR and NOT gates, while NAND, NOR, XOR and XNOR are commonly used derived or special-purpose gates. Boolean laws and identities can be used to simplify logical expressions, making circuits more efficient and reducing their complexity. Therefore, Boolean algebra provides an important foundation for understanding how computers and other digital devices perform logical operations.
